**Bounce** is an iPhone app I am building. Most sports apps want a form first, then a plan picked
from a menu, and then they follow the plan whatever happens. I wanted the opposite: an app that
already knows the whole context. The sports I did over the years, what I want now in my own words,
my injuries and how they healed. From that, it only tells me the next one to three things to do.

There is one input bar, and no forms. I type or talk to it: "I feel a bit sick since Sunday", "the
knee is fine again", "I want to run with friends when it is sunny". I can also add a photo, a scan
of a doctor's letter, or a file. Voice is transcribed on the phone, so the audio never leaves it.

The Today screen is the mascot, one sentence, my goals, and the next sessions. They land in free
slots of my calendar, when the weather is good for outside, and at real places: the gym I pay for,
the club, the park where I usually run. Each card shows the shape of the session, and one tap sends
it to the Apple Watch or the calendar.

Every morning it compares my heart rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep with my own
normal, not with an average person, and says if today is for going hard or going easy. When
something hurts, it asks one question a day, "How's your right shoulder today?", from 0 to 10. When
I say I am sick, it shows up in my health as "probably a cold", and the plan stays easy until I say
I am better.

When it is not sure, it asks instead of guessing: "In June 2024 you had an ankle X-ray. What
happened?" I answer in a sentence, or point it to the report. It only opens a report attached to an
email when I tap.

The You tab has my trends, with gaps shown as gaps and the device each line comes from. Below that,
what it learned about me as short facts, each with where it comes from, and my injuries and
illnesses with their dates and how far along the recovery is.

It reads from many places. Apple Health, and through it the watch, Garmin, WHOOP or Oura. Strava.
Gmail and Google Calendar, where it finds the gym I pay for, the races I entered, and the physio
visits around an old injury. The iPhone calendar, the weather, my everyday movement, the music I
play on runs, and my photos, where it spots the sports in old pictures. Tap any source to see what
it reads, why, and what Claude gets. Turn it off and everything it gave is forgotten.

The data stays on the phone, in one file and in the phone's own backup. To think, it sends a
written summary to Claude, through a small relay that keeps and logs nothing. No exact location, no
email addresses, no photos. One screen shows the whole path after each read: what was read, what is
kept, the exact text that was sent, and what came back.

It is in early testing on TestFlight, and for now I am the only tester.
